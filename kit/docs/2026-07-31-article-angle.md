# The article angle: "why you should focus NOW"

Written Jul 31 after JC reframed the job. The article is not trying to prove
"niching causes growth" as a universal law. It is aimed at ONE group: **broad
agencies that have stopped growing.** The giants (Wunderman, Merkle, DEPT) are
broad because they CAN be, not because broad works — and they are excluded from
the study anyway. Agencies like Buro Bruut (broad, +200%) are fine and are not
the target either.

## The four segments (n=257 with growth data)

| Segment | n | share | no proof | cannot be booked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| broad + growing | 79 | 31% | 61% | 81% |
| **broad + flat/shrinking** | **75** | **29%** | **75%** | **91%** |
| focused + growing | 48 | 19% | 58% | 73% |
| focused + flat/shrinking | 55 | 21% | 67% | 75% |

**The target group is 75 agencies — 29% of the market.** Of those:
- 56 show zero numeric proof
- 68 cannot be booked
- 61 never name who they serve
- **40 have all three problems at once**

## The finding that makes the article work

Look again at rows 1 and 2. The broad agencies that ARE growing have almost the
same website as the broad agencies that are stuck: 61% vs 75% no proof, 81% vs
91% unbookable. Nearly identical.

**If the website were driving the growth, those two rows would look different.
They don't.**

So the growth in row 1 is coming from somewhere that is not the website —
referrals, the founder's network, existing clients expanding, timing. Which is
exactly why positioning shows no correlation with headcount growth across the
whole population: in this market most growth is network-driven, and the network
is orthogonal to the homepage.

That reconciles everything and it is the argument JC actually needs:

> The stuck agencies are not a different species from the growing ones.
> They are the same agency, later. Row 1 is running on a network that still
> works. Row 2 is what it looks like when that network runs out — and when it
> does, there is nothing on the website to catch anyone.

That is "why focus NOW": you fix it while referrals are still carrying you, not
after they stop. Fixing it takes months; the run-out is not announced.

## The supporting proof points

**1. They are saying the same sentence.** Across all 327 positioning lines, the
most common words are: marketing (17%), online (14%), bedrijven (12%), merken
(11%), helpen (9%), ambitieuze (5%), groeien (5%). 38 agencies (12%) write a
literal variant of "wij helpen [ambitieuze] bedrijven groeien". Of those 38,
17 have no proof, 25 cannot be booked, 18 never name a buyer.

A stuck founder can be shown a stack of competitor homepages that are
interchangeable with their own. That lands harder than any statistic.

**2. Three of four agencies cannot be booked.** 25% have a booking link. And
this is the one metric that tracks growth cleanly: 57% growing with a booking
link, 46% with a contact form, 19% with neither. Regex-detected (Calendly /
Cal.com / booking phrases), not a model judgement — nobody can argue it.

**3. Nobody has proof.** 66% show zero numeric client results even after we
scraped their case-study pages directly. Only 41% name an ideal client anywhere.

## What NOT to claim

- Do not say niching causes growth. It failed three honest tests
  (2-axis, 7-axis, sharpness); correlations are -0.03 to -0.05.
- Do not use Buro Bruut or TNG as cautionary tales. They are broad AND growing.
  They are the "your network still works" example, not the failure case.
- Do not present the four segments as a maturity ladder. We cannot yet show
  agencies moving between them — that needs the Wayback history run.

## The one claim still worth testing

If the Wayback run shows that today's stuck agencies were *also* broad five
years ago, while the focused-and-growing ones narrowed at some point, the
"same agency, later" story gets a time dimension and becomes much stronger.
That run is currently throttled by archive.org and resumable.

Even without it, the argument above stands on the cross-section: the growing
and the stuck broad agencies have the same website, so the website is not what
separates them — yet — and that is the entire point.
