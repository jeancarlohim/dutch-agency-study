# The lifecycle test — did today's broad agencies earn it?

JC's objection: the cross-section may be misleading, because giants like DEPT and
Merkle are broad TODAY but got there by proving themselves in a niche first and
then scaling horizontally. If that path is common, a snapshot would show broad
agencies winning while positioning still causes growth.

Testable only with history. So: 330 domains pulled from the Internet Archive,
1,018 snapshots, median 6 years of observed history per agency, then 46 Sonnet
agents classified each agency's positioning at every point in time.

Crucially, this classification includes **niche-to-broad** — the DEPT path. The
earlier Rotterdam scheme had no such value and therefore could not have found it.

## Result 1 — the lifecycle explanation is mostly dead

Of 241 agencies with clean history (domains that changed hands excluded):

| Path | n | share |
|---|---|---|
| **always-broad** | **147** | **61%** |
| always-niche | 41 | 17% |
| broad-to-niche (narrowed) | 30 | 12% |
| **niche-to-broad (the DEPT path)** | **11** | **5%** |
| unclear / oscillating | 12 | 5% |

**61% have been broad for their entire recorded history** — a median of six
years, some going back to 2005. They are not former specialists who earned the
right to broaden. They never chose in the first place.

Only 5% took the DEPT path. So the confound is real but small: it cannot explain
away a 61% majority. In this population, broad is not a graduation. It is a
default.

That is a strong sentence for the article, and it is now evidenced:

> DEPT and Merkle are broad because they can afford to be. These 147 agencies
> are broad because they never decided.

## Result 2 — the agencies that narrowed look different

| Path | n | % growing | median growth | median proof | **has a booking link** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| always-broad | 126 | 49% | **0%** | 0 | **16%** |
| always-niche | 33 | 45% | 0% | 0 | 18% |
| **broad-to-niche** | **25** | 52% | **+15%** | 0 | **36%** |
| niche-to-broad | 9 | 56% | +4% | 0 | 22% |

Two things stand out for the group that narrowed:

1. **Median growth +15%, against 0% for every other path.** The share that grow
   at all is barely different (52% vs 49%) — the difference is in how much.
2. **They are twice as likely to be bookable** (36% vs 16%). Narrowing does not
   arrive alone. It comes with operational tightening.

**Caveat, stated plainly:** n=25, and causality could run backwards — an agency
that is already growing can afford to start saying no. This is directional
evidence, not proof.

## Result 3 — proof timing does NOT support proof causing growth

| | n | % growing | median |
|---|---|---|---|
| numeric proof visible by 2022 | 29 | 52% | +4% |
| proof only from 2023 onward | 28 | 57% | +17% |
| never showed a number | 146 | 47% | 0% |

Having proof beats never having it (52-57% vs 47%). But **earlier proof is not
better than recent proof** — recent is better. That is the signature of proof
FOLLOWING wins, not causing them. Agencies publish the numbers they just earned.

Do not claim proof drives growth. Claim that the ones who are winning can show
receipts and the stuck ones cannot.

## The single best artifact in the dataset

**Dapper's pivot was one word.**

- 2022: *"We maximize growth for impactful brands"*
- 2025: *"We maximize growth for impactful B2B brands"*

Pivot year ~2024. Result: +132% headcount, 28 numeric case studies, the highest
score in the study. One word, and everything downstream changed.

Other narrowings worth using:

| Agency | Was | Now | 2yr |
|---|---|---|---|
| mPower Digital | "for ambitious entrepreneurs" | "for ambitious e-commerce companies" | +66% |
| Suwemo | "growth through content marketing outsourcing" | "impactful content for IT and sales professionals" | +100% |
| WEARE | "helping bold brands stand out" | "brand identity for tech scale-ups" | +33% |
| GETTING THE MARKET | generic marketing concepts | maritime, shipbuilding, logistics | +28% |

And the honest counter-examples, which must be included: the agency that
narrowed to "e-commerce partner for €1M+ revenue businesses" is **-13%**, and
SEM agency narrowed in 2025 and is **-9%**. Narrowing is not a guarantee.

## What the article can now say

1. **61% of these agencies have never been anything but broad** — median six
   years of evidence. Broad was not earned, it was defaulted into.
2. **The ones who narrowed grow at +15% median while everyone else sits at 0%,
   and are twice as likely to let you book a call.** Directional, n=25.
3. **Dapper changed one word and everything followed.**
4. Still true and still the strongest single number: three of four agencies
   cannot be booked, and bookability tracks growth 57% / 46% / 19%.

## What it still cannot say

- That niching causes growth. The cross-sectional correlations remain -0.03 to
  -0.05, and the path data is n=25 on the key cell.
- That proof causes growth. Timing says the reverse is more likely.
- Anything about DEPT-scale agencies. They were excluded from the study, and
  only 5% of this population took that path anyway.
