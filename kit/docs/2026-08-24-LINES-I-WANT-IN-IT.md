**Backed by:** C1 (narrowing null in ~30 cuts) + A1 (44% vs 26%, +19pp, p=0.0015).

**Use 388.** That is the population the six frozen tests ran on — your own RUNLOG line for 7 Aug says so.

**Why 256 is smaller, and why it doesn't weaken you.** The 4-client gate. An agency showing one or two clients can't be scored for proof or focus — with one case the top bucket is 100% by arithmetic, and that number means nothing.

You already closed this objection. You simulated the gate at 3, 2 and 1 (C2). Same answer at every threshold.

So if anyone asks why 256 and not 388, the reply is: *I ran it their way too. Nothing moved.*

**Possible origin of 1,586:** 85% Wayback coverage on 1,830 domains lands around 1,555. Close, but it isn't a number that exists in any file. Don't use it.

**Also:** "Tangible proof does" needs the D1 hedge close by, or line one is a causation claim. Your own rule from 10 Aug: *travel together, never drives.*

## 6. The credibility setup

**Verbatim:**
> Over the last 2 years I consumed almost every piece of content of Blair Eens and David C Baker. Everything from value driven pricing, positioning, productization and all. So I was quite convinced that I was going to back their words.

**Tightened:**
> For two years I read everything Blair Enns and David C. Baker put out. Value-based pricing. Positioning. Productization. All of it.
>
> I went into this study expecting to back them up.

**⚠️ Spelling:** it's **Blair Enns**, not Eens. Both are real, named public figures with audiences in this market. Getting the name wrong in a piece about rigour costs you more than the line is worth.

**Why this line is doing the heavy lifting:** it converts the null from "JC disagrees with the experts" into "JC tried to confirm the experts and couldn't." That's the difference between a contrarian take and a finding.

It also pre-empts the obvious attack: *you only found this because it suits your offer.* The answer is in the sentence — you expected the opposite.

**Where it goes:** the intro, right before or after line 1. It sets up the disappointment that makes the whole piece honest.

**One caution:** you're not claiming Enns or Baker are wrong. The study measured headcount growth over two years in one market. That is not the thing they claim. Say so — see D2, D8. It costs you nothing and it stops a fight you don't want.
