# Insights to bring over — Proof Agency Article

Everything worth carrying into the spine, pulled from the same 14 chats (2–10 Aug 2026).

Each item is marked:

- **SOLID** — survives the study's own rules (40 per side, gap over 10 points, correction for 30 declared tests)
- **DIRECTIONAL** — real signal, cells under the floor. A lead, not a claim.
- **DEAD** — tested, null. Say so out loud.
- **RETRACTED** — was published internally, then collapsed. Do not print.

---

## A. The findings that carry the article

### A1. Tangible proof separates growing from stuck — SOLID

44% growing vs 26% without. +19pp, p=0.0015, n=256, after window.

Replicates in two cohorts that share no agencies:

- Narrower cohort: 51% vs 25%, p=0.015
- Control cohort: 42% vs 24%, p=0.017

It is the only finding that survives correction for the 30 declared tests.

### A2. The comparison is not proof vs no proof. It is tangible vs intangible — SOLID

Nobody with 4+ clients shown has *no* proof. The third group is empty.

- 112 agencies with at least one number: 46% growing
- 144 agencies with names, logos and quotes only: 24% growing

p=0.0007.

**The line:** every agency has proof. The growing ones have proof you can measure. The flat ones have proof you have to take on faith.

This removes the escape route. "I have plenty of social proof" — he does. It's the kind that didn't move.

### A3. It is a threshold, not a dose — SOLID (collapsed comparison only)

| Numbered cases | n | % growing |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 144 | 24% |
| 1 | 29 | 55% |
| 2 | 14 | 50% |
| 3–4 | 17 | 35% |
| 5–9 | 24 | 46% |
| 10+ | 28 | 39% |

Zero → 24%. One or more → 46%, p=0.0008.
Exactly one 55% · two to four 42% · five or more 42%, p=0.35.

The whole effect is the first case. Individual bands are under the floor — only publish the collapsed version.

**The line:** the thing you've been putting off because it felt like a big project is a one-afternoon job.

### A4. Intangible proof is linked to standing still, not declining — SOLID

| Case studies | n | Declining | Flat | Growing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Contain a number | 112 | 33% | 21% | 46% |
| No numbers at all | 144 | 27% | **49%** | 24% |

Decline rates are nearly identical. The whole gap sits in the flat column.

From the other direction: 49% of declining agencies publish a number. 59% of growing. Only 26% of flat.

Numbers separate moving from not-moving — not up from down.

Survives the size control at 1–5 and 6–15. Reverses at 16+.

**The detail to use:** flat agencies with no numbers publish a median of 22 case studies. They aren't lazy. They're publishing plenty and none of it says what happened.

### A5. Claims only work with proof underneath — SOLID (best corner vs worst)

| | n | % growing |
|---|---|---|
| Number on site + hero names a client type | 54 | **54%** |
| Number on site + hero silent on who | 57 | 37% |
| Faith-based proof + hero names a client type | 76 | 26% |
| Faith-based proof + hero silent on who | 67 | **22%** |

Best vs worst: 54% vs 22%, p=0.0004.

Inside the intangible group, saying who you serve changes nothing: 26% vs 22%.
Inside the numbered group it's 54% vs 37% (p=0.09, suggestive).

**The line:** naming your client on the homepage is worth nothing by itself. The claim only pays when there's a measurable result underneath it.

This is the clean version of what the retired alignment table was reaching for — with cells big enough to stand on.

### A6. It is a small-agency effect — SOLID, and it's a constraint on every claim above

| Size | With result | Without | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | 24% | 8% | +16pp |
| 6–15 | 49% | 34% | +15pp |
| 16+ | 55% | 76% | −21pp |

Size dominates everything. Agencies of 6+ grew 45–48% whether or not they showed proof.

Never write "for everyone". Always "especially small agencies".

### A7. Old proof predicts. Current proof reflects. — SOLID

After window (site as it was two years ago): 46% vs 24%.
Today window (site as it is now): 41% vs 30%, p=0.10, not significant.

Proof measured *before* the growth window tracks growth. Proof measured after mostly reflects it.

This is also the honest limit — see D1.

## B. The counts. No growth data needed, nobody can attack them.

### B1. The market publishes almost no numbers

- 91% of 31,868 case studies contain no number
- ~1.5% name revenue
- Recomputed across the full 688-agency set: 15,028 cases live today, 1,267 carry a number — roughly **8%**
- Independent replication in the untested cohort: 6,278 cases, 10.9% carry a number

The figure got *worse* with more coverage.

### B2. Proof is extremely concentrated — the sharpest count in the study

Volume is ordinary. Proof is not.

- Top 5% of agencies hold **52%** of all numbered cases
- Top 10% hold 72%
- **68% of agencies hold none at all**

Thirty-four agencies own half the proof in the Dutch agency market.

**The contrast is the story:** the effort is evenly distributed. The evidence is not. Everyone is writing case studies. Almost nobody puts a result in one.

### B3. What a "case study" actually is in this market — the format finding

| Form | Count | Share | Carries a number |
|---|---|---|---|
| Summary tile | 8,295 | 55% | 10% |
| Logo only | 3,129 | 21% | **0.03%** |
| Quote / testimonial | 2,322 | 16% | 5% |
| Detailed tile | 1,181 | 8% | 24% |
| **Full case page** | **101** | **0.7%** | 24% |

Across 688 agencies and 15,028 items there are **101 full written case studies in the entire Dutch agency market.**

One logo out of 3,129 had a number attached.

**The reframe:** the gap isn't laziness. It's format. A logo grid physically has nowhere to put a result. Agencies chose a layout and the layout decided what they could prove.

**The line:** 15,028 case studies on 688 Dutch agency websites. 101 of them are actually written up. The rest are logos and tiles — and a logo cannot carry a result.

### B4. The 101 are 73 accidents, not a disciplined minority

- 63 of 73 agencies wrote exactly **one** full case page
- Only 4 wrote more than two
- Top writer wrote 9

And writing a full page doesn't mean putting a number in it — achieve.nl wrote 5 full pages across 63 cases with zero numbers anywhere.

**The line:** nobody in this market writes them at all, including the people who wrote one.

Note: many of the deepest writers are solo operators, not shops.

### B5. The stuck segment — 124 agencies

Today window, ≤15 staff, 4+ clients shown, zero numbers, 2y growth ≤ 0.

- Median 3 people, range 1–15
- Median 6.9 years staff tenure — settled teams, not startups
- 79 exactly flat, 45 shrinking *(correction: the README calls them "flat"; the filter includes shrinking)*
- Median 12 clients shown, median 18 case studies each
- **3,016 case studies across the segment. Zero numbers in any of them.**
- 62 are already narrow. 34 scattered. 27 too thin to score.

**Half this segment did the positioning work. It didn't help, because the proof underneath is empty.**

**The line:** 124 Dutch agencies, median three people, published 3,016 case studies between them. Not one contains a number. Half of them have already niched.

**Caution before printing:** "zero numbers" partly reflects extraction. 40% of agencies were flagged truncated by source, so counts are floors. Say "3,016 *or more*" and it's unassailable.

### B6. The market is moving — and this is the one that makes stuck agencies feel it

286 agencies with cases in both windows. Growth removed entirely — pure market movement, no causation problem.

- 193 had zero numbers before
- **36 added a tangible result — 19%**
- 157 still zero — 81%

Market total: 33% showed tangible proof before → 40% after.

Stickiness: 93 already had numbers. 79 kept them. Only 14 dropped to zero.

**The line:** 1 in 5 agencies without a single measurable result added one in the last two years. The market is moving. 157 have not.

### B7. The old-and-small extreme

Old, small shops: **6%** show a single measurable result.

### B8. Bookability — the one metric that tracks growth cleanly

- Booking link: 57% growing
- Contact form only: 46%
- Neither: 19%

25% of agencies have a booking link. Three of four cannot be booked.

Regex-detected (Calendly / Cal.com / booking phrases), not a model judgement. Nobody can argue it.

### B9. The crowded sentence

Across 327 positioning lines the most common words: marketing 17%, online 14%, bedrijven 12%, merken 11%, helpen 9%, ambitieuze 5%, groeien 5%.

38 agencies (12%) write a literal variant of *"wij helpen [ambitieuze] bedrijven groeien"*. Of those 38: 17 have no proof, 25 cannot be booked, 18 never name a buyer.

**Why it lands:** a stuck founder can be shown a stack of competitor homepages interchangeable with his own. That beats any statistic.

### B10. Coverage collapses as you move from the agency to the buyer

Share of 20,746 deduped client entries that state each axis:

| Axis | Coverage |
|---|---|
| What they sell | 73% |
| Client industry | 31% |
| Platform / channel | 22% |
| Problem they fix | 18% |
| Where clients are | 16% |
| Money model | 7% |
| Client size | 5% |

**Agencies describe their own work. They rarely describe the buyer.**

Only 41% name an ideal client anywhere. 66% show zero numeric client results.

### B11. No industry owns the market

Top client industry is Construction & real estate at 9.4%. Top six together are only 48%.

The market-wide picture is flat. The per-agency picture is fragmented. That's why the growth test by client type found nothing — no industry group ever reached 8 agencies.

### B12. The biggest bucket is the emptiest

| Axis | Top buckets | Clients | With a result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Client size stated | Omvang in cijfers | 344 | **21.5%** |
| Platform | Social organisch, Website & CMS | 1,345 | 16.4% |
| Industry | Construction, Hospitality, Healthcare, Retail | 2,267 | 16.3% |
| Money model | B2B | 515 | 13.4% |
| Problem | Merk & verhaal, Website verouderd | 948 | 10.9% |
| Geography | Nederlandse stad of plaats | 1,247 | 10.4% |
| **What they sell** | **Website & webdesign, Branding** | **5,481** | **5.3%** |

Selling design produces no measurable claim. Describing the client, and the rate triples.

### B13. Narrowing is rare

Of 241 with clean history: 61% always-broad, 17% always-narrow, 12% narrowed later, 5% broadened.

At n=1,830: only 8.5% ever narrowed. 62% stayed broad.

### B14. Starting narrow is a young behaviour

**Not one agency older than 15 years in the study started narrow and stayed narrow.**

Young agencies: 40% growing vs 27% for 15y+.

Caveat that makes it safe: "always-narrow" means narrow in every snapshot. An old agency has 15+ years of snapshots — one broad year kills the label. Part of the pattern is measurement.

Safe wording: *"Starting narrow is a recent behaviour. Among agencies older than 15 years, not a single one began focused."*

## C. The nulls — the contrarian half

### C1. Narrowing does not move growth — DEAD, in ~30 cuts

All six frozen tests null. Then: size bands, age bands, category cuts, an eight-cut confound grid, a fourteen-feature sweep, the pre-written "old + broad + no proof = stuck" prediction (32% vs 34%), and age (young/old both null, narrow slightly worse in both).

Correlations run −0.03 to −0.05. **The direction is consistently negative** — narrow agencies grew slightly less at every threshold.

### C2. The 4-case gate is not why — DEAD

Lowering the scoring gate from 4 to 1:

| Gate | Narrow % growing | Scattered | Gap | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 28% | 38% | −10 | 0.13 |
| 3 | 31% | 38% | −6 | 0.30 |
| 2 | 31% | 38% | −7 | 0.26 |
| 1 | 31% | 38% | −7 | 0.22 |

The answer doesn't move. **This is the reply if anyone says you excluded too many agencies: you ran it their way, same answer.**

(The artefact it exposes: all 167 extra agencies land in "narrow", because with one or two cases the top bucket is 100% by arithmetic.)

### C3. Narrowing does not move money either — DEAD

Revenue per head, 68 narrow vs 54 scattered: $150,000 vs $157,670. Gap −$7,670, p=0.86.

Caveat: 111 of 170 agencies sit in just two revenue bands, so revenue per head is largely headcount wearing a different hat.

### C4. The kind of number does not matter, on a clean cut — DEAD

Money or leads number: 62 agencies, 47% growing.
Softer numbers only: 50 agencies, 44%.

Three points, p=0.85. Clears the group sizes.

**This contradicts the exploratory ladder (money 54% vs reach 32%). Do not print the ladder.** Second exploratory finding in the study to fall apart on a better cut, after the say/do table.

### C5. Proof concentration by axis — DEAD and unpublishable

| Proof concentrated in… | Concentrated | Spread | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Client industry | 20 → 50% | 25 → 36% | 0.38 |
| What they sell | 29 → 45% | 30 → 40% | 0.80 |
| Platform / channel | 30 → 43% | 17 → 35% | 0.76 |

Every group under 40. Structural reason: only 70 agencies publish 4+ numbered cases. Split that and you're below the floor before you start.

Needs agencies with 8+ numbered cases — there are only 38.

### C6. Detail on its own produces nothing — DEAD

Any detailed tile or full page: 33% growing. None: 35%. p=0.80.
Mostly logos: 38% growing. Not logo-heavy: 33%. p=0.62.

Logo-heavy agencies did marginally *better*.

**The correction to make:** detailed tiles are more likely to *contain* numbers, which is partly circular — a tile is coded "detailed" because it says more, and saying more includes saying a result. Format removes an obstacle. It does not create the result.

**Say it this way:** detail is not the point. It's the container.

### C7. The full 12-feature screen — nothing on the website survives

296 agencies, today window.

| Feature | High half | Low half | Gap | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bigger agency** | 50% growing | 17% | +33 | <0.0001 |
| **Longer staff tenure** | 15% growing | 54% | **−39** | <0.0001 |
| Has a numbered case | 41% | 30% | +10 | 0.10 |
| Narrow vs scattered | 28% | 37% | −9 | 0.16 |
| Clients shown | | | +4 | 0.46 |
| Cases published | | | +2 | 0.71 |
| Case specificity | | | +3 | 0.63 |
| % logos | | | +3 | 0.60 |
| % testimonials | | | −7 | 0.22 |
| Industry focus | | | −1 | 1.00 |
| Craft focus | | | +1 | 1.00 |

**Every website feature sits between −9 and +10.**

But both survivors are self-measuring. Tenure = years since people joined, so low tenure means recent hiring, which *is* growth. Size means grew already.

**The honest summary: in this data, headcount growth correlates with headcount.**

### C8. Crowded bucket, not narrow vs broad — DIRECTIONAL, just under the floor

Agencies whose main product is a website or brand identity: 27% growing (n=101).
Agencies selling something else: 36% (n=125).

Gap 8–9 points, stable at min-3 and min-4 clients. The noise floor is 10.

Six of seven axes point the same way: sitting in the crowded bucket goes with *less* growth. That's a crowding effect, not a niching effect.

## D. The honest limits — say them before anyone else does

### D1. Reverse causality is unresolved and unresolvable here

This is the after window. Agencies that were already growing had results to publish. In the today window the gap shrinks to +10pp and stops being significant — exactly what you'd expect if some of it is reverse causality.

**Defensible version:** *positioning didn't move growth in any of thirty cuts. The one thing that did was having a single client result with a number attached — mostly among small agencies, and I can't prove which way the arrow points.*

Wording rule already on record: "travel together", never "drives". "Separates", never "creates".

### D2. Headcount is a weak proxy — four challenges, one serious

1. **37% of everything counted as "growing" is a single person joining.** 48% of agencies have five staff or fewer; median is 6. At a three-person shop one hire is +33%.
2. **"Flat" is partly a floor effect.** 38% added or lost exactly zero people.
3. **LinkedIn counts profiles, not employees.** Dutch small agencies run heavily on freelance capacity. None of it appears.
4. **Two years is short** for a positioning change. Half the narrowers pivoted in 2023–24.

What holds: all 388 agencies have the full 25 months, no partial series. Monthly series used, so leavers counted correctly. Percent *and* people added reported throughout.

**How to handle it:** concede first, don't defend. A noisy measure makes a null *easier* to explain. Where it bites is the proof finding — that's a positive result and needs the measure to hold.

### D3. The usable sample is smaller than the headline suggests

688 agencies have narrowness status, 706 have case data. Only 374 can ever appear in a growth test. After the 4-client and known-growth filters the real working number was **256**.

**Stating this makes you harder to attack, not weaker.**

### D4. The 297 have no growth data at all

| Cohort | Has growth | Missing | % missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| control | 240 | 23 | 9% |
| narrower | 134 | 12 | 8% |
| original340 | 0 | **297** | **100%** |

The LinkedIn headcount scrape only ever covered the narrower/control matched pairs.

They have today window only — no before, no after, no say-side snapshots.

### D5. No past case studies exist. Full stop.

The 97 narrowers have current-day case studies (1,150 extracted from 8 pages per site). The Wayback layer is **homepage text only, 3,000 chars, 2–4 timepoints** — no case extraction has been run on it.

Even if extraction were run, cases that lived on /work or /cases in the past aren't in a homepage snapshot. Any "previous case study" number would be an undercount, not a count.

**So hypothesis 2 as a *change* claim ("narrowers grew in proof") has no data behind it. Only the *level* claim is available.**

The 455-run at `study-outreach/outputs/2026-08-02-historical-proof/data/` was built to fix exactly this — 155 narrowers + 300 city-matched always-broad controls, case-page discovery then raw text at pivot−1 and pivot+2.

### D6. No city column exists

The 45 Rotterdam agencies exist as a documented count (295 national + 45 Rotterdam = 340). The only geo field describes where the *clients* are.

**No Rotterdam-specific cut is computable from this kit.** The fix is one `origin` column added to measures_v2.csv.

### D7. Extraction is a floor, always

40% of agencies flagged truncated by source. The error runs one way — it under-reports proof, never over-reports.

Dapper proves the point: their tiles carried numbers but no service text, so extraction found almost nothing to tag. They landed "not measurable" and they're one of the best-proven agencies in the study.

### D8. What the data cannot give at all

Pricing. Win rate. Revenue per head with a real instrument. Whether narrowing pays in margin rather than headcount.

**Say it out loud in the article.** It's the honest limit and it's the reason the next study exists.

## F. Directional leads — real signal, cells too small

### F1. Money proof beats visibility proof

Among the 112 agencies with numbered proof:

| Proof consists of | n | % growing |
|---|---|---|
| Money only (revenue, leads, conversion) | 20 | **70%** |
| Other metrics only | 14 | 50% |
| Soft only (reach, views, traffic, SEO) | 28 | 43% |
| Mixed | 50 | 36% |

Money-only vs soft-only: +27pp, p=0.08.

Shuffle test: 20,000 random regroupings produced a 27-point gap about 1 time in 12. (The proof finding, for comparison, was 1 in 2,500.)

**Oddity worth noticing:** mixed is the *worst* cell. One sharp money number beat a dashboard of everything.

**On branding specifically:** there is no branding metric because branding results almost never carry numbers. Branding proof lives in the intangible group, which grew at 24%.

Related earlier cut (after window, n=256), same shape:

| Shows at least one… | n | Growing |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue / sales | 38 | 55% |
| Ad performance | 21 | 52% |
| Traffic | 52 | 40% |
| Leads / inquiries | 44 | 39% |
| SEO positions | 17 | 35% |
| Conversion | 27 | 33% |
| Reach / views | 40 | 32% |

Agencies showing rankings sit near the bottom. **SEO proof is not the winner.**

Sharpest version: money/leads only, no vanity metrics — 68% growing (n=25). Vanity only — 46% (n=24). Both mixed — 33% (n=49).

### F2. The proof switch — adding proof correlates harder than having it

286 agencies with cases in both windows, 249 with 1y growth.

| Path (before → after) | n | Growing |
|---|---|---|
| Intangible → tangible | 35 | **46%** |
| Stayed intangible | 131 | 17% |
| Always tangible | 70 | 24% |
| Tangible → intangible | 13 | 23% |

**The switchers beat agencies that had proof all along.**

Size-controlled (6+ band): switched 48%, stayed intangible 35%, always tangible 32%.

Switch cell is 35 — under the floor.

**And it's a separate signal from repositioning.** Of the 35 switchers, 24 *broadened* their message, 6 narrowed. Switched proof + narrowed: 1/6 growing. Switched proof + didn't narrow: 15/29 (52%).

The growth does not come from repositioning. Dapper is one agency where both switches happened at once — an anecdote on top of the pattern, not the pattern.

### F3. Proof clusters vertically

217 agencies have at least one tangible case. **68.7% of those have 2 or more.** It's not a one-off.

Extra tangible cases cluster in the same niche:

| | Tangible cases | Baseline (any case) |
|---|---|---|
| Same industry, avg match | 60.5% | 47.7% |
| Same industry, ALL same | 25% | 10% |
| Same craft, avg match | 62.9% | 56.2% |
| Same craft, ALL same | 23% | 10% |

Vertical clustering shows the bigger jump. Industry sample is 64 agencies, craft 118 — both thin.

Where agencies stack 2+ tangible cases: Retail & e-commerce 14 · Construction/real estate 7 · Business services 7 · Fashion 4 · Software 3 · Hospitality 3 · Transport 3.

### F4. E-commerce proof

Three separate windows, same direction, none clears the bar:

- After window: tangible 42.3% growing (2y) vs 29.9%. Tangible side has 26 with growth data.
- Today window: tangible sample drops to 9.
- Combined: tangible 43.8% vs 26.4%. n=32 vs 91.

**Largest e-commerce sample this dataset can give. Strong lead, not a finding.**

### F5. Creative agencies are not exempt

| Work type | n | Flat | Growing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Creative (branding, print, video) | 103 | 46% | 31% |
| Website & app build | 82 | 30% | 34% |
| Performance (ads, SEO, e-com) | 33 | 30% | 36% |

The proof gap survives inside every type:

| Work type | Proof | Flat | Growing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Creative | no numbers | 60% | 18% |
| Creative | has numbers | 28% | **48%** |
| Build | no numbers | 50% | 25% |
| Build | has numbers | 12% | 43% |
| Performance | no numbers | 35% | 18% |
| Performance | has numbers | 25% | 56% |

Creative rows are n=57 and n=46 — both clear the floor.

**What kills the "creative work can't produce numbers" excuse:** performance agencies publish numbers at 48%. Creative at 45%. Almost identical. Both groups mostly don't.

**A branding agency that publishes numbers grows at 48%. One that doesn't grows at 18%. Same work, different page.**

### F6. Leverage vs labour — the two-population idea holds, but not the way it first looked

Split on headcount from two years ago (the honest version):

| | Growing | Median size then |
|---|---|---|
| Leverage (high revenue/head) | 41% | 5 people |
| Labour (low revenue/head) | 38% | 13 people |

Proof, re-split:

| | With a result | Without |
|---|---|---|
| Leverage | 48% (n=25) | 39% (n=49) |
| Labour | 36% (n=22) | 40% (n=55) |

**Proof travels with growth in the leverage half. In the labour half it's flat or slightly negative.**

That asymmetry is the interesting bit — pure reverse causation would show up in *both* halves.

Concentration stays null in both. Splitting did not rescue niching.

Cells 22–55, below the floor.

## K. The mechanism question — still open, and I know it's open

**My question:** what is the mechanism by which proof travelled among the agencies that are growing?

**What the data can't do:** it contains websites and headcounts. Zero buyers. Nobody in the dataset was ever observed choosing an agency.

**What the data does do — it kills rival explanations:**

- Dapper grew with **zero named clients**. Anonymous numbers can't circulate through anyone's network. So it isn't a network-distribution mechanism.
- Narrowing is null in 30 cuts. If proof spread through vertical buyer networks, focus would amplify it. It doesn't. **No network road.**
- Proof only helps small agencies. Big ones win on name. So it acts exactly where trust is scarcest.
- No-proof agencies go **flat, not backward**. Old clients stay. New buyers pick someone else. Top of funnel only.

**The leading hypothesis (label it as a bet, not a finding):** the proof doesn't travel. It waits. A buyer gets 3–5 names, opens the sites, compares. 91% show projects. 9% show a result. The number wins the comparison.

**Three explanations the data cannot separate:**
1. Proof helps win clients (supported only by the 35-agency switch cell)
2. Growth produces proof (nothing rules it out)
3. A third factor drives both — measurement culture

**The standard B2B route, for the section that needs it** (general knowledge, not this data):
1. **Trigger** — nobody browses for agencies. Something breaks. Agency relationships turn over every 2–3 years.
2. **Self-diagnosis** — the buyer frames the problem as a channel before talking to anyone.
3. **Longlist** — ask peers (dominant in NL) · memory (95:5 — most of the market isn't buying at any moment) · Google.
4. **The silent filter** — buyers complete 60–70% of the journey before any contact. Minutes per site. Cut 5–6 down to 2–3. **This is the only step that is silent, solo and website-based — the only place a published number acts without the agency doing anything.**
5. **Conversations** — 2–3 intro calls. In NL the "klik" carries unusual weight.
6. **Proposal or pitch.**
7. **Decision** — founder/MD for SMB. Chemistry first, evidence second, price third.

**The test that would settle it:** 10–15 interviews across three cells — growers *without* proof (26% of the no-proof group), proof-holders that *didn't* grow (56% of them), and the switchers. Two questions each: where did your last five clients first hear of you, and when did they see your cases.

## L. Positioning decisions I made along the way

### L1. The finding is not what the study set out to prove

Original goal: prove narrowing drives growth. It didn't, in ~30 cuts.

**The authority is in publishing that anyway.** Almost nobody in this market publishes a result that costs them.

Stacking that works: **lead with the null → then the mirror → then the threshold as relief.** The obvious finding stops competing for novelty and becomes the payoff.

### L2. "You need proof" is not a new insight — and that's the point

*"Not a single competent agency owner will argue this."*

Correct. So the piece isn't "here's why proof matters." It's: **you already know this. Here is the measured distance between what you believe and what is on your site right now.**

A mirror, not a lesson. Mirrors get replies.

### L3. Do not claim causation

*"To say that if I pop one proof on the website it creates clients — that's an easy causation-not-correlation issue."*

The timing test proves the point: recent proof beats early proof, which is the signature of proof following wins.

**Defensible version:** the growing agencies can show receipts. The stuck ones cannot. Proof as a marker of a working agency, not a lever you pull.

**And it changes what the article sells:** the gap is not "add a number." The gap is that most agencies have results they never captured in measurable form. The work is extracting proof that already exists.

### L4. The market-movement section needs no growth data

Removing growth entirely (B6) gives the emotional punch with zero causation exposure. *Their peers are doing it. They're being left behind.*

### L5. Wording rule

Say **"tangible result"**, not "case study with a number" — matches the canonical term on record.

Report agencies, not pairs. No acronyms. Correlation wording only.

### L6. Proof is one lever of four

*"Proof is ONE LEVER of conversion. What calls do is find who to talk to, how good the offer is, how much credibility you have, and how well you did the problem-implication-solution. A coach can have many things going wrong. And proof lives on the delivery calls, not the discovery — the discovery call just tells me how good the proof is."*

So the study proves one dial at market scale. The call reads all four.

The ending turn for each article: *"Proof is one lever. A sales call tests four — the buyer, the offer, the credibility behind it, and the call itself. Most founders never check which one is failing."*

(Note: "autopsy" is on the banned-morbid list for public copy. Use "full diagnostic" or "X-ray".)

### L7. Coaches front, agencies background

Own data backs the sequence. The mismatch cell was the worst position in the study — naming a market your cases don't prove.

Claiming agencies publicly today, with zero agency cases, puts me in that exact cell.

Two flags: check the coach proof carries **numbers** (the finding is tangible proof, not proof in general). And set a switch trigger now — 2–3 agency cases with a number flips agencies from background to frontend. Otherwise "background" runs forever.

The agency outbound list is already built: the 124.
